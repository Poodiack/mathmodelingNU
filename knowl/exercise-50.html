Exercise 3.

Consider a cup of coffee that is initially \(100^\circ\)F, cools to \(90^\circ\)F in 10 minutes, and sits in a room whose temperature is a constant \(60^\circ\)F. A simple assumption for how the coffee cools is that the amount the temperature changes from one minute to the next is proportional to the difference between the coffee temperature and the room temperature.

That is,
\begin{equation*} a_{n+1} - a_{n} = k (a_n - 60) \end{equation*}
where \(a_n\) is the coffee temperature at minute \(n\) and \(k\) is some constant.
(a)

Implement this model to predict the coffee temperature for \(0\le n\le 150\text{.}\) Use a slider to find the value of \(k\) that yields \(a_{10} \approx 90\text{.}\)

(b)

Create a graph of \(a_n\) vs \(n\text{.}\)

(c)

Does this model seem reasonable? Briefly explain why or why not.
